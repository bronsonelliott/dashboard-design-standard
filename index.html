01 Purpose and scope

A dashboard puts the essential signals for a recurring decision on one screen. Its readers can see what is happening, spot what needs attention, and decide whether to act or investigate.

Start with the monitoring task. Keep the signals that change a decision, give each one enough context to interpret it, and put the most consequential information where the eye lands first. Treat the dashboard as the front door to deeper work. Few’s dashboard writings provide the foundation.

What the reader needsBuild this
Check a known set of signals repeatedly and decide whether to actA dashboard. Keep the essential signals, comparisons and exceptions visible together.
Read a narrative, reconcile an official figure, or audit complete rowsA report with commentary, fixed reporting scope, and the appropriate detail or export.
Ask new questions, change dimensions, or test competing explanationsAn exploration workspace with coordinated views and inspectable detail. Link to it from the monitoring screen when the same audience uses both.
Navigate different jobs, edit records, assign work, or complete a sequenceA multi-page app with clear navigation and task-specific screens. Give it a dashboard landing view only if there is a recurring monitoring job.
PUR-01 MUST

Name the audience, recurring decision and action. Keep a first-screen item only when the brief identifies the decision it supports.

PUR-02 SHOULD

Move exhaustive records, open-ended exploration and multi-step workflows to the appropriate companion surface. Link from the relevant summary.

PUR-03 GUIDANCE

Start a requirements meeting with a recent decision. Ask what evidence made someone act, and sketch only the information they used.

PUR-04 MUST

Name each screen in the brief as a monitoring view, a report or an exploration screen, by the reader’s job. Review a report or exploration screen against the rules that carry over to it in the tables below, and record the rest as not applicable to that surface.

02 How to use this standard

For a new build, choose the type, write the brief, agree on measures, and sketch the screen before configuring charts. For a review, use the release checklist and cite the rule ID in each finding. Every numbered rule has a direct link on its ID.

MUST
Release gate. Fix any failure before shipping. When a rule truly does not apply, record the condition that makes it inapplicable.
SHOULD
Expected practice. Record a reason, tradeoff and mitigation for each departure, and have the reviewer agree before release.
GUIDANCE
A suggested technique. Apply it when it helps the reader’s task.

How levels are assigned. Team addition A rule is MUST when breaking it can mislead a reader, hide a signal the decision needs, block access for some readers, or skip a release control. A rule is SHOULD when it is a strong default with legitimate exceptions, or when a MUST rule elsewhere already enforces its core. A rule is GUIDANCE when it calls for judgment rather than a pass-or-fail check. Give new rules a level with the same test.

Attribution. Few supplies the monitoring method, the emphasis on comparison and visual hierarchy, and the compact chart patterns. Team addition marks an implementation choice, an extension, or a release policy specified here. The IDs and rule levels are this standard’s own. The dashboard labels in §03 are used as a working classification. Every example, figure and dataset is original and invented.

Walk through these questions in order

05 Measures and context

Choose the measure closest to the decision. Count late dispatches when the job is to prevent delays. Use a proxy only when a direct measure is unavailable, and explain what the proxy misses. Separate the observed value, the comparison and the action threshold.

MEA-01 MUST

Label every displayed measure with its unit and time scope. Give decision measures a named comparison or explicitly state that no valid comparator exists and why.

MEA-02 MUST

Match the comparator on definition, population, time grain and elapsed period. Label deliberate differences, such as comparing a partial actual with a future target, and show pace or a labeled projection where the decision depends on being on track.

MEA-03 MUST

Compute rates from their underlying numerator and denominator at the displayed grain. Apply status and thresholds to full precision. Distinguish actual zero, missing, insufficient, partial and stale values.

MEA-04 MUST

When assigning a rating, define the direction of improvement, inclusive and exclusive cutoffs, owner and action. Use words with a distinct mark and give unknown or stale its own state. For an inherited cutoff with no documented owner, follow MEA-08.

MEA-05 SHOULD

Pair summaries with the exception, spread or segment that can change the decision. For a short ranked list, state the sort, count shown and total or link to the full list.

MEA-06 SHOULD

Set the default period in the brief. Where none is set, open on the latest complete period at the decision’s grain, never on all periods combined. Name the period on screen, and show an in-progress period only with its elapsed portion labeled and a comparator matched under MEA-02.

MEA-07 MUST

Show a stock measure, such as open orders or headcount, as a balance at a stated as-of date. A period selection sets that date and must not drop items that began earlier. Do not sum balances across periods.

MEA-08 MUST

Before release, resolve every inherited rating or color cutoff that has no documented owner. Get it approved under MEA-04, remove it, or keep it labeled as provisional with its cutoffs and direction stated, a named person seeking approval and a review date within 90 days. Remove it if that date passes without approval.

A target is the desired outcome, a forecast is an estimate, a prior period shows observed change, and a norm describes typical behavior under a stated method. Name the comparator directly. For forecasts, show material uncertainty with documented bounds or a range and say what the range means. Compare the same weekdays or elapsed hours where seasonality or shift timing matters.

Stock measures

Team addition A flow measure counts what happened during a period, such as orders received in October. A stock measure counts what exists at a moment, such as open orders or headcount on 31 October. Stock measures break in one specific way. A period filter applied to the start date keeps only the items that began in that period, so the balance looks far smaller than it is (MEA-07).

  • State the as-of date beside every stock value.
  • Let a period selection set the as-of date. Use the period’s last day, or the data-as-of time for the current period. Items that started earlier and are still open stay in the count.
  • Sum a stock across categories, never across periods. Over a span of periods, show the closing balance or an average with its method stated.
  • In a trend, plot one balance per period end.
  • Show movement with separate flow measures, such as opened and closed in the period, or with a bridge from opening to closing balance.

06 Choose a visualization

Start with the comparison. Use a number for reading one value, a table for exact lookup, aligned marks for comparing magnitudes, and time or space only where order or location matters. Reuse the same display for the same task.

VIS-01 MUST

Use a labeled scale that preserves the encoded relationship. Every length bar starts at zero, including each signed bar at the visible zero line. A bridge step may float from its running total under DSP-17. A line or dot may use a labeled focused scale if it preserves the intended comparison.

VIS-02 MUST

For panels intended to compare magnitudes, align units, time windows, category order and scale domains. If the task compares shapes on independent scales, label each scale and the purpose.

VIS-03 MUST

Show missing observations as gaps or explicit states. Distinguish forecasts from actuals with a named boundary and a different line style or mark. Use verified intervals when uncertainty could change the decision.

VIS-04 GUIDANCE

Choose the simplest display that answers the question. Use aligned position or length before area, and separate series into panels when a combined view obstructs comparison.

Chart gallery

Team addition Chart-by-chart defaults below turn the visual principles into reviewable tests. The geographic and treemap rules set narrow conditions for using area and position.

Single value

Use when. The first task is to read one current number or status.

Avoid when. Readers need to compare a set of values or judge a trend.

Common mistake. A large number with no threshold or period.

DSP-01 SHOULD

Pair the value with a name, unit, period and decision context.

Bullet graph

Use when. One featured value needs comparison with a target or benchmark.

Avoid when. Readers primarily need trend shape, or the comparison mixes units or populations.

Common mistake. Using a cropped baseline for a length bar or adding arbitrary performance bands.

DSP-02 MUST

Use a common linear scale, a prominent featured mark and a short perpendicular comparison marker. A length bar starts at zero. Use a point for a focused scale. Include bands only for approved cutoffs and state their direction in text.

Sparkline

Use when. A nearby value needs a compact history trace.

Avoid when. The reader needs to estimate intermediate values or compare different scales precisely.

Common mistake. Auto-scaling comparable rows independently or leaving the ending date unclear.

DSP-03 SHOULD

Place the sparkline beside its current value. State the time window and grain, mark the latest point and share scales where magnitudes are compared.

Bar chart

Use when. Rank or compare magnitudes across categories.

Avoid when. Shape through ordered time or precise comparison on a focused scale is the primary task.

Common mistake. Truncated bars or alphabetical sorting that obscures rank.

DSP-04 MUST

Start the value axis at zero. Keep lengths comparable and sort by value unless a meaningful category order should take precedence.

Line chart

Use when. Read movement or compare trajectories over ordered time.

Avoid when. The x-axis is unordered categories or only exact values matter.

Common mistake. Connecting across missing periods or evenly spacing irregular dates.

DSP-05 MUST

Use equally spaced positions for regular reporting buckets and elapsed-time positions for irregular observations. Show the first and last periods, units and a labeled scale. Leave gaps where data is missing.

Combination bar and line

Use when. Compare a discrete actual to a commensurate benchmark over time.

Avoid when. The measures need different units or independent scales.

Common mistake. Using two y-axes or allowing the benchmark to look like a second actual.

DSP-06 MUST

Use one labeled scale and compatible units and time grain. Start bars at zero. Distinguish the benchmark line by style and label.

Stacked bar

Use when. Show the whole and a few additive parts.

Avoid when. Readers must compare interior segments precisely or the parts overlap.

Common mistake. Stacking rates with different denominators or omitting the total behind a 100% view.

DSP-07 MUST

Stack mutually exclusive parts on a common zero origin. Keep segment order stable. State the denominator for 100% stacks and directly identify the parts.

Dot plot

Use when. Compare closely spaced values on a common numeric scale.

Avoid when. Absolute magnitude from zero is the central comparison.

Common mistake. Changing domains across panels or making a tight range look like an absolute scale.

DSP-08 SHOULD

Use one row per category and a labeled numeric axis. Show exact values when a focused domain is needed.

Histogram

Use when. Read the shape and tail of one numeric distribution.

Avoid when. Readers need individual values or compact comparison across several groups.

Common mistake. Treating numeric bins like sortable categories or using unequal bins with raw count heights.

DSP-09 MUST

Use contiguous equal-width numeric bins and a zero count baseline. State the bin width and observation count. State how boundary values are assigned.

Box plot

Use when. Compare median and spread across groups on the same scale.

Avoid when. Exact shape, multimodality or a very small sample drives the decision.

Common mistake. Calling every whisker a minimum or maximum or hiding sample size.

DSP-10 SHOULD

State the box, whisker and outlier definitions. Give each group’s sample size and use a shared scale.

Scatter plot

Use when. Assess a relationship between two numeric variables at one observation grain.

Avoid when. One variable is a nominal category, or the task is to show ordered change.

Common mistake. Joining independent observations or treating correlation as causal.

DSP-11 SHOULD

Name both axes with units and name the observation grain. Show overlap with an explicit method when it obscures the pattern.

Small multiples

Use when. Compare the same pattern or magnitude across categories without overlapping series.

Avoid when. The groups require incompatible units or the panels become unreadably small.

Common mistake. Using independent axes for a magnitude comparison.

DSP-12 SHOULD

Repeat the same chart structure, encoding, date range and ordering across panels. Apply VIS-02 to every scale.

Table

Use when. Look up exact values, names, dates or records.

Avoid when. Spotting a broad pattern or ranking is the first job and a chart communicates it faster.

Common mistake. Using a dense grid or mixing units in one column.

DSP-13 SHOULD

Use explicit headers and units, a known sort order and right-aligned numbers. Add an adjacent visual only when it supports a distinct task.

Treemap · restricted

Use when. Screen many nested, positive, additive parts for dominant groups.

Avoid when. Close comparisons, negative values or temporal order matter. Prefer bars for shallow hierarchies when space allows.

Common mistake. Using both saturated color and area for the same quantity or placing a zero value in a visible rectangle.

DSP-14 SHOULD

Size each rectangle by its share of the parent. Label hierarchy, units and values. Use boundaries and provide a readable ranked table or list.

Signed deviation bar

Use when. See which categories are over or under a known reference and by how much.

Avoid when. The absolute level matters more than the difference, or groups use incompatible references.

Common mistake. Displaying a signed difference without naming the comparator.

DSP-15 SHOULD

Use one visible zero reference. Show sign and units directly and name the baseline.

Point map · restricted

Use when. Distance, location or a barrier changes the response.

Avoid when. The place name is only a grouping label.

Common mistake. Assigning random positions or hiding values in pointer-only marks.

DSP-16 SHOULD

Use verified spatial coordinates, show orientation or scale where relevant, and state the decision supported by location. Include a text equivalent.

Bridge (waterfall) · restricted

Use when. One additive measure moves from a start total to an end total, and the reader needs to see which steps explain the difference. Plan to actual and opening to closing balance are typical.

Avoid when. The steps are rates, ratios or averages, which do not add up. Also avoid it when every step is small beside the totals. On a scale that starts at zero those steps shrink to slivers, so show them as signed deviation bars and state the two totals as numbers.

Common mistake. Cutting the axis to enlarge the steps, or relying on red and green to tell increases from decreases.

DSP-17 MUST

Use a bridge only to explain the difference between two totals of one additive measure. Draw the start and end totals as bars from zero on one unbroken scale. Float each step from the running total, label it with its signed value, and make the steps sum to the difference. Tell increases from decreases without relying on color.

08 Layout and arrangement

Screen, grid and the single-screen principle

Design for the viewport agreed in the brief, after browser and product chrome. Everything essential fits that one screen at default zoom, with no scrolling or paging (LAY-01). Build every layout on a shared grid. The starting grid below is a team addition.

Importance and viewing order

Readers of left-to-right languages start at the top left. Put the most important item there, give it the most space, and let importance fall off toward the bottom right (LAY-03). Number the regions in the wireframe, and keep the reading order in the page markup the same as the viewing order.

Grouping, whitespace and borders

Group related items by proximity and alignment first. Use whitespace to separate groups. Add a light surface fill only when proximity is not enough, and never box every chart (LAY-02).

Filters and controls

Put shared controls in one quiet band beside the heading, or in a left rail. Show their current state as text, with a reset control (LAY-04, INT-01).

Detail pages and drill paths

Detail lives on drill-through pages reached from a specific item. Each detail page names its scope, keeps the filter context and returns to the same overview state (LAY-05, INT-02).

“View by” switches

10 Typography and number formatting

TYPF-01 SHOULD

Use one readable sans-serif family. At the design viewport, start at 24–32 CSS px for the page title, 24–36 for primary values, 16–20 for section titles, 14–16 for data and controls, and 12–14 for secondary notes and ticks. TYPF-02 SHOULD

Right-align numeric table cells within a column and use tabular digits where supported. Keep the same units, precision and sign convention within a comparable series or column.

TYPF-03 MUST

Display units and time labels without ambiguity. Use the dashboard’s declared locale and currency. Apply display rounding only after calculation and preserve exact source precision in authorized detail when the decision requires it.

TYPF-04 MUST

Use one style for negative numbers across a dashboard, either a minus sign or parentheses. State what the sign of each variance means, either the arithmetic difference or better/(worse), and apply that meaning to every row, including cost lines where lower is better.

11 Text, titles and labels

TXT-01 SHOULD

Title each chart with its monitoring question or a supported current finding. If the title states a finding, update it when filters, refresh or missing data invalidate it.

TXT-02 MUST

Name every axis, series and marker that would otherwise be ambiguous. Prefer direct labels when legible. If needed, place a compact legend beside the plot with matching order and non-color cues.

TXT-03 MUST

Show current scope, latest successful data-as-of time and stale or partial state where the reader makes the decision. Put full definitions, sources and cutoff notes in a labeled footnote or linked definition.

TXT-04 SHOULD

Use the business’s own terms in plain language. Define each acronym at first use on the dashboard or in its linked definitions.

Write for the job. “Dispatches at risk by site” works for a stable monitor. “East has 7 of 12 at-risk dispatches” works if the title follows the data. A short annotation can name a verified event or explain a discontinuity. Anchor it to the affected observation and distinguish observed timing from a proven explanation.

Keep chart labels horizontal where possible. Thin tick labels before shrinking them. State units once per axis or column where that makes every value clear. Keep critical thresholds and warnings next to the affected value, even if the detailed method lives in a note.

Titles that state the point

Topic only“Revenue”Avoid. The reader has to read the chart to learn anything (TXT-01).

Direct labels before legends

Label the mark, not a key (TXT-02). A line gets its name at its right end, a bar gets its value at its tip, and a dot gets both. Use a legend only when direct labels would collide, and place it beside the plot in the same order as the marks. The reader should never travel from mark to key and back more than once.

Annotations

Annotate only the one or two points that change how a chart reads, such as a policy change, an outage or a data gap. Keep each note to a few words in the secondary text color, anchor it to the affected observation with a short leader line, and never cover a mark. Say what happened, not what the number is. The number already has a label.

Footnotes for definitions and freshness

Every dashboard carries a definition note for any measure whose name does not fully define it, and a freshness note with the data-as-of time and refresh behavior (TXT-03).

13 Accessibility

Team addition Apply WCAG 2.2 AA where the delivery surface supports web content, and verify equivalent access on other surfaces. The checks here cover common dashboard barriers. See the accessibility reference for the complete criteria.

ACC-01 MUST

Operate every control with a keyboard. Use a logical focus order, visible and unobscured focus, and a way to leave each control. Preserve focus during refresh and return it predictably after dismissing a panel.

ACC-02 MUST

Give each chart a meaningful accessible name and provide an equivalent readable summary or data table. Expose control names, values, states and table headers. Announce meaningful changes without reading every refresh.

ACC-03 MUST

Keep text and controls usable at 200% text resize. At 320 CSS px width or equivalent 400% zoom, reflow content in reading order without page-level horizontal scrolling. Where a chart or data table genuinely requires two dimensions, contain its scrolling and provide an equivalent readable alternative.

ACC-04 MUST

Verify contrast and non-color cues, complete the core tasks by keyboard, and read the overview, changed filters and detail using a supported screen reader. If the chart surface cannot provide equivalent access, include an accessible route before release.

ACC-05 MUST

Make hover or focus disclosures dismissible, hoverable and persistent where WCAG 1.4.13 applies. Provide a visible or equivalent touch path for the same information.

At narrow widths prioritize the same critical information and permit vertical reading. A responsive stack can preserve access without changing the purpose of the desktop overview.

16 Glossary

3-D chart
A display that uses perspective or depth to draw marks. The added dimension can distort comparisons in a monitoring view.
Actual
A value observed in the stated period and population.
Additive measure
A measure whose parts can be summed to a meaningful total at the stated grain.
Aggregation
Summarizing underlying observations as a total, count, rate or other statistic.
Alert
A signal or notification that a defined condition needs someone’s attention.
Analytical monitoring
Routine checking for known deviations or segments that warrant investigation.
Annotation
A short note tied to a specific observation or event in a display.
Axis
A reference line or labeled direction that locates data values or categories.
Bar chart
A chart that encodes numeric magnitude with bar length from zero.
Baseline
A reference used for a comparison. On a bar chart, the visible origin from which length is measured.
Benchmark
A named comparison point or standard, such as a target, prior result or typical value.
Better/(worse)
A variance signed by its effect on the result. Favorable differences are positive and unfavorable ones are negative or in parentheses, whichever way the raw numbers moved.
BI (business intelligence)
Tools and practices for turning organizational data into information used in decisions.
Bin
A defined interval used to group numeric values in a histogram.
Box plot
A distribution summary with a median, quartiles and whiskers. It can show outliers as separate marks.
Bridge (waterfall)
A chart that walks from a start total to an end total through signed steps that float from the running total.
Bullet graph
A compact linear display of a featured value, optionally with comparison markers and performance bands.
Category
A named group such as a site, task or region.
Color-vision deficiency
Reduced ability to distinguish some colors. Protan, deutan and tritan describe different patterns of reduced color discrimination.
Combination chart
A display that puts more than one mark type on the same plot, such as bars and a line.
Companion surface
A report, exploration or detail screen that sits beside a monitoring view in the same app.
Comparator
The named target, prior result, estimate or norm against which a measure is evaluated.
Contrast ratio
The ratio of perceived relative luminances, computed using WCAG’s formula with a 0.05 offset, expressed as a ratio such as 4.5:1.
Control
An element a reader operates, such as a selector, link or reset button.
Control state
A condition such as focused, selected, expanded, disabled or invalid.
Correlation
A statistical association between numeric measures. It cannot on its own show that one causes the other.
CSS pixel
A logical web layout unit. Its relationship to physical screen pixels changes with device and zoom.
CST (Central Standard Time)
The North American standard time zone UTC−06:00 used in the dispatch examples.
Dashboard
A single-screen monitoring view of the essential information for a recurring decision.
Data-as-of time
The time through which the data is known to represent the source. It can precede the last dashboard reload.
Deduplication
Recognizing a repeated record or notification and handling it once under a defined rule.
Denominator
The eligible count or quantity by which a rate, proportion or share is divided.
Deviation
A signed difference between an observed value and a named reference.
Dimension
An attribute used to group or filter data, such as site or period.
Direct label
Text next to the mark or series it describes.
Direct measure
An observation or calculation that measures the outcome the decision actually concerns.
Distribution
The pattern of values in a set, including spread, concentration and tails.
Diverging scale
A color scale with two directions away from a meaningful midpoint.
Donut chart
A pie-like ring that encodes shares by arc angle.
Dot plot
Here, a chart that places a point for each named category on a shared numeric axis.
Drill-down and drill path
Moving from summary to detail at a finer grain, and the route used to reach it.
Dual-axis chart
A chart that overlays measures against two independently scaled numeric axes in the same direction.
Elapsed period
The portion of a reporting period that has passed, such as the first two hours of a shift.
Encoding
The assignment of data to a visual property such as position, length, shape or hue.
Essential graphical boundary
An edge or mark someone must see to understand a visual or identify a control.
Exception
An observation that meets a named condition requiring attention, such as a threshold breach.
Exploration workspace
A set of views for asking and testing changing questions about data.
Filter
A control or condition that limits the population or period included in a view.
Fiscal period
A reporting period from the organization’s financial calendar.
Flow measure
A measure of what happened during a period, such as orders received in a month. It can be summed across periods.
Focus and focus order
The element that receives keyboard input and the sequence in which elements receive it.
Forecast or projection
An estimate of a future value based on stated data and assumptions.
Freshness
How recent the data is relative to the decision and the agreed limit on delay.
Grain
The level represented by one observation or plotted point, such as one shift or one site-day.
Grid and gutter
A set of aligned columns and the spacing between them.
Gridline
A reference guide aligned to a scale tick.
Hex code
A six-digit base-16 notation for a color’s red, green and blue components, such as #17242C.
Histogram
A chart of counts or density across contiguous numeric intervals.
Hue
The color family, such as blue or green, apart from its lightness or intensity.
Index baseline
A reference assigned a fixed value, often 100, to compare relative changes in measures with different units.
Interquartile range (IQR)
The third quartile minus the first quartile, or the width of the middle half of the distribution.
Interval or bounds
Lower and upper limits shown with an estimate. Their interpretation depends on the method used.
Key performance indicator (KPI)
A measure selected to track progress toward an important objective.
Legend
A key that explains the meaning of a mark, style or color.
Line chart
A chart that connects observations in a meaningful order, usually time.
Linear scale
A scale on which equal numeric differences occupy equal visual distances.
Mark
A visible element that represents data, such as a point, bar or line.
Measure
A defined numeric value such as a count, amount, duration or rate.
Median
The middle value of ordered observations, or the average of the two middle values when the count is even.
Metric owner
The person accountable for a measure’s definition and approved use.
Midpoint
The meaningful center of a diverging scale, such as zero deviation from plan.
Missing value
A value that is unobserved, unavailable or undefined. A genuine zero has a different meaning.
Multimodality
The presence of more than one peak in a distribution.
Norm
A documented description of typical behavior, such as a median over comparable shifts.
Numerator
The count or quantity divided by the denominator to compute a rate or share.
Observation
A measured or recorded unit at the stated grain.
Operational dashboard
A monitoring view that supports intervention in an active process.
Outlier
An observation beyond a stated statistical rule. It may be a valid observation.
Partial period
A reporting period that is still in progress or incompletely loaded.
Percentage point (pp)
The arithmetic difference between two percentages, expressed in points.
Percentile
A value at or below which approximately a stated share of observations falls, under a specified calculation.
Pie chart
A circle partitioned into sectors to show shares of a whole.
Point map
A display of observed positions in geographic space. Relative position is part of the evidence.
Population
The entities or observations eligible under the displayed rules and scope.
Precision and rounding
The resolution shown for a number, and the rule for reducing it to that resolution for display.
Projected coordinate grid
A flat coordinate system that places locations on a map at a stated scale.
Provisional threshold
An inherited cutoff shown with a label and a review date while it waits for an owner’s approval.
Proxy
An indirect measure substituted for a direct outcome when the outcome is unavailable.
Qualitative band or rating
A named interpretation of a numeric range, such as Watch.
Quartiles
The 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles. Q1 and Q3 bound the middle half of a distribution.
Radar chart
A chart that puts values on spokes around a center and connects them into a shape.
Radial gauge
A circular or semicircular scale with a needle or arc showing a current value.
Rate
A numerator divided by a denominator, often expressed as a percentage or per unit of exposure.
Reflow
Rearranging content as available space shrinks or text grows, preserving reading order and access.
Refresh cadence
How often data is updated. The maximum acceptable data age can differ from the refresh interval.
Relative percent change
The change divided by the starting value, expressed as a percentage where the starting value supports that interpretation.
Report
A screen or document for reading complete, fixed-scope figures or records, as opposed to at-a-glance monitoring.
Sample size (n)
The number of observations contributing to a statistic or view.
Scale domain
The range of values covered by a chart axis or color scale.
Scale tick
A marked position on a scale, often with a numeric or time label.
Scatter plot
A chart that locates each observation by two numeric variables.
Scenario range
A span of outcomes from specified assumptions. It has no statistical coverage level unless one is established.
Scope
The period, population, filters and other boundaries that define what is shown.
Screen reader
Software that presents interface content and structure through speech or braille.
Seasonality
A pattern that recurs on a calendar or operational cycle.
Sequential scale
An ordered color scale for low-to-high magnitude.
Series
A set of related marks showing one measure or grouping across categories or time.
Signed value
A number whose plus or minus sign expresses direction relative to zero.
Small multiples
Repeated charts with the same structure, each showing a different group or measure.
Sparkline
A compact time trace displayed alongside a value, usually with few or no axis labels.
sRGB
A standard red-green-blue color space used for these hex colors and contrast calculations.
Stacked bar
A bar split into additive parts. A 100% stacked bar scales the sum of all parts to 100%.
Stale data
Data older than the approved limit for a decision.
Stock measure
A balance counted at a moment, such as headcount or open orders on a date. It can be summed across categories but not across periods.
Strategic dashboard
A monitoring view of progress toward longer-term objectives.
Tabular figures
Digits of equal width used to keep numeric values aligned.
Tactical dashboard
A manager’s view, refreshed daily or weekly, that shows which areas drive a result and what to adjust this week. A team addition to the three types Few describes.
Tail
The thin or extreme end of a distribution.
Target
A desired level or threshold for a defined measure, population and time.
Threshold
A boundary that changes interpretation, status or action under a stated rule.
Time grain
The reporting interval of an observation, such as hour, day or month.
Tooltip
A short disclosure shown on hover, focus or tap, typically for supporting detail.
Treemap
Nested rectangles whose areas represent additive quantities.
UTC offset
The signed difference between a local time zone and Coordinated Universal Time.
Variance
In this standard, the business difference from a comparator, rather than the statistical measure of spread.
View-by switch
A local control that re-cuts one display by a different dimension without changing its measures, period or scope.
Viewport
The visible content area available to the dashboard.
Visual hierarchy
The relative prominence of screen elements and the order it suggests.
WCAG 2.2 AA
The W3C accessibility guidelines, version 2.2, at conformance level AA.
Whisker
A box plot line extending from a quartile to a defined limit or observation.
Whitespace
Unoccupied space used to separate groups and give content room to read.
Wireframe
A simple screen plan showing the size, position and purpose of each region.
Zero reference
The axis line or tick that separates positive from negative values, or anchors the length of absolute bars.
