What the numbers mean

Definitions used on this screen.

  • Headcount: active employees on the last day of the month.
  • Attrition: exits in the last 12 months ÷ average headcount over the same months.
  • Regrettable: exits the manager marked as a loss the company wanted to avoid.
  • Time to fill: days since an open role was approved, averaged across open roles.
Bullet graphs: black bar = actual, blue tick = plan or target. Attrition bands: lighter = better.
Status for attrition, where lower is better: ● Within limit 14% or less, ◆ Watch 14–17%, ▲ Act over 17%.
Hires and exits exclude internal transfers between departments.
Detail: role-level open requisitions and exit reasons on linked pages, same filters. All values are invented.

The brief

AudienceHR business partners and department heads, in the monthly workforce review.
DecisionWhere to add recruiting capacity, and which departments need a retention plan this quarter.
TypeTactical: a manager view refreshed monthly, between strategic plans and daily operations (section 03).
ComparisonsHeadcount and hiring plan. Attrition target set by the people team.
CutoffsAttrition: Within limit at 14% or less, Watch 14–17%, Act over 17%.

How this applies the standard

  • The headcount line uses a focused scale, and the subtitle says so. Bars always start at zero (VIS-01).
  • Hires and exits share one zero line instead of a floating waterfall (DSP-15).
  • Lower-is-better attrition has bands, a target and words, not just a red number (DSP-02, MEA-04).
  • Definitions sit on the screen, not in a separate document (TXT-03).
  • Scope is explicit: employees only, with contractors reported elsewhere (MEA-01, PUR-02).
  • No individual names or employee rows on the overview. Detail sits behind access control (LAY-05).

About the data

  • A made-up software company of about 2,500 employees in five departments.
  • Every number is invented, but they add up: monthly hires and exits roll the headcount forward, and the departments sum to the company totals.
